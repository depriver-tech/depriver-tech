# Session 05 | Reports and evidence

## What you should be able to do
Separate observation, interpretation and recommendation in a short report.

## Preparation and session plan
Recall the key definition and one example from the previous session before continuing. Allow about 75 minutes, including an independent attempt and correction. This is a proposed study session, not a university timetable.

## Core notes and worked example
Follow the structure required by the assignment. A typical lab report includes title, objective, relevant background, apparatus or software, method, results, discussion, conclusion and references. Results state what was observed or calculated. Discussion interprets the result, considers errors and limitations, and compares it with the intended model. The conclusion answers the objective using the evidence actually collected.

Worked example: A resistor experiment might report nominal resistance 1.0 kOhm and measured resistance 0.98 kOhm. The absolute difference is 0.02 kOhm; relative to the nominal value it is 2%. This is not automatically a malfunction. The discussion needs resistor tolerance, instrument resolution and test conditions. Do not invent measurements to make a report look complete.

A reproducible method names enough detail for another person to repeat it. For a program, record source-file version, compiler flags, test inputs and outputs. For a circuit calculation, show the diagram, assumptions, equations, substitutions and units.

## Reasoning in more depth
A report makes its reasoning inspectable. Explain the question, method, conditions, results and limitations before recommending action. Report the sample size and how observations were collected. An average without variability or context can hide important failures. If a test used one computer, do not generalize to the whole campus. Recommendations should follow the evidence and state what further information would change the decision.

## Independent task | Attempt before reading the answer
In a hypothetical test, 4 of 20 login attempts failed on one lab PC. Write a result, a limitation and a proportion calculation.

## Worked answer and marking points
Result: Four of twenty observed attempts failed, an observed failure proportion of 4/20=0.20 or 20%. Limitation: attempts came from one machine under one set of conditions; they do not establish the campus-wide rate or the cause. Recommendation: repeat a documented test across other machines and times before assigning a cause or estimating wider impact.
