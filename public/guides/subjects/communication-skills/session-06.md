# Session 06 | Summarizing, paraphrasing and citing

## What you should be able to do
Paraphrase accurately while retaining attribution and uncertainty.

## Preparation and session plan
Recall the key definition and one example from the previous session before continuing. Allow about 75 minutes, including an independent attempt and correction. This is a proposed study session, not a university timetable.

## Core notes and worked example
A summary selects the main points and is shorter than its source. A paraphrase restates a specific idea using your own sentence structure and wording. Neither removes the obligation to credit the source when the idea came from someone else. A quotation preserves the exact words and must be marked and cited; use quotations sparingly.

A practical workflow is read, close, write, compare, cite. Closing the source makes it harder to merely exchange a few words. Compare for accuracy, then add the source using the required referencing style. Keep the author or organization, title, year where available, URL or publication information, and any page or section needed to locate the claim.

Worked example: If an operating-systems textbook explains virtual memory, a report can cite that textbook for the concept and separately label your own diagram and example. Do not claim that a generated explanation is a verified source. Check factual claims against reliable material and follow the institution's rules on assistance and academic integrity.

## Reasoning in more depth
Paraphrasing is a reconstruction of meaning, not substituting synonyms in the same sentence frame. Read, close the source, express the idea for your purpose, then compare for accuracy and add a citation. Preserve qualifications such as may, in this sample and under these conditions. A summary compresses several points; a direct quotation reproduces exact wording and requires quotation marks plus attribution.

## Independent task | Attempt before reading the answer
Paraphrase this original practice sentence: In this classroom trial, students who attempted retrieval questions recalled more definitions one day later. Identify a claim the sentence does not justify.

## Worked answer and marking points
Paraphrase: The classroom trial found better next-day definition recall among the students who used retrieval questions. Cite the trial if using a real source. The sentence does not show that every student benefits, that understanding improves indefinitely, or that retrieval caused the difference without knowing the study design. A careful paraphrase retains the limited setting and measured outcome.
