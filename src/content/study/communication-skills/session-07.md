## What you should be able to do
Write an actionable email and record a collaborative decision clearly.

## Preparation and session plan
Recall the key definition and one example from the previous session before continuing. Allow about 75 minutes, including an independent attempt and correction. This is a proposed study session, not a university timetable.

## Core notes and worked example
Use a specific subject, a courteous greeting, a concise context, a clear request and a close. Supply the details the recipient needs, but avoid unnecessary personal information. Send a follow-up after a reasonable interval appropriate to the deadline; do not interpret silence as approval.

Worked example:

Subject: CS 8138: clarification on file-input format

Dear Lecturer,
I am working on the file-processing exercise. The brief names a marks file but does not state whether each line contains one mark or a student name followed by a mark. Could you confirm the required format? I have attached the relevant part of the brief for context.
Thank you,
Privatus Cosmas

For group work, record decisions, owners and due dates. "Someone will finish the report" is not an assignment. "Asha will draft the method by Friday; Juma will check the results table on Saturday" makes responsibility visible. Use actual agreed names and dates, not assumptions.

## Reasoning in more depth
A useful subject line states the topic and request. Give enough context to let the recipient act, provide a realistic deadline with a reason and identify attachments by purpose. In group work, record who will do what and when, then confirm unresolved questions. A polite tone does not require a long introduction. Avoid exposing private grades, account details or personal records in a public group message.

## Independent task | Attempt before reading the answer
Draft an email asking a lecturer to clarify whether a report needs individual or group submission. Use a placeholder deadline rather than inventing an actual course rule.

## Worked answer and marking points
Subject: Clarification requested: report submission format. Dear Lecturer, our group is preparing the report and would like to confirm whether each student should submit separately or whether one group submission is required. Could you clarify before [planning date] so we can organize the final files? Thank you, [name/module]. This asks one concrete question without assuming a rule.
