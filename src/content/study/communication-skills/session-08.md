## What you should be able to do
Structure a short talk and respond to feedback using observable criteria.

## Preparation and session plan
Recall the key definition and one example from the previous session before continuing. Allow about 75 minutes, including an independent attempt and correction. This is a proposed study session, not a university timetable.

## Core notes and worked example
A presentation needs a clear question, a small number of supporting points and a conclusion the audience can remember. Begin with the problem, show one concrete example, then explain the result. Slides should support speech rather than reproduce an essay. Label diagrams and keep enough contrast and text size for the room.

Practise to time. A three-minute explanation might allocate 30 seconds to context, 90 seconds to the example, 40 seconds to interpretation and 20 seconds to the takeaway. These are planning proportions, not compulsory rules. Rehearse with a timer and adjust to your actual pace.

Worked example: Present the integer-division bug by showing the expected average, the original expression, its actual output and the corrected expression. Ask the audience to predict the result before revealing it. During questions, restate the question to check understanding. Say what you know and what needs checking; guessing is not professionalism.

Useful feedback describes the artifact and its effect: "The graph lacks units, so I cannot interpret the slope." It is more actionable than "This is bad." Listen without interrupting, clarify the requested change and decide how to revise.

## Reasoning in more depth
A short presentation needs a purpose, an opening question, a small number of connected points and a clear ending. Rehearse aloud with a timer. Use slides to support an explanation rather than to display a full script. While listening, record the speaker's claim before preparing a response. Feedback helps when it identifies an observable behavior, its effect and a practical next step; a label such as boring gives no repair path.

## Independent task | Attempt before reading the answer
Plan a three-minute explanation of what an operating system does. Add one audience question and one specific feedback criterion.

## Worked answer and marking points
Plan: 30 seconds for a familiar app-opening example; 90 seconds for process, memory and file abstractions; 40 seconds for a diagram connecting the app to hardware; 20 seconds for a recap. Ask: Why can two applications each behave as if they have their own memory? Feedback criterion: Can a listener explain the role of the kernel without reading the slide? Adjust pacing using an actual timed rehearsal.
