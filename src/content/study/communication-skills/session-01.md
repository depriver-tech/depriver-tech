## What you should be able to do
Adapt a technical message to a specific audience and required action.

## Preparation and session plan
Read the module preparation notes and identify any prerequisite you need to revise. Allow about 75 minutes, including an independent attempt and correction. This is a proposed study session, not a university timetable.

## Core notes and worked example
Before writing, answer three questions: Who will read this? What do they already know? What should they understand or do afterwards? A lecturer assessing a lab needs a reproducible method and evidence. A classmate trying to install a compiler needs ordered instructions and a way to verify success. A manager approving a change needs the problem, options, consequences and recommendation.

A channel should fit the purpose. A short email works for a clear request; a diagram can explain relationships; a report records evidence; a live conversation helps resolve ambiguity. Do not assume more technical vocabulary makes writing more professional.

Worked example: "The application has issues" gives no decision-useful information. A clearer defect report is: "On the registration page, submitting an empty email field creates an account instead of showing a validation message. Expected: reject the form and explain the missing field. Reproduced in three attempts." It separates observation from expectation without inventing a cause.

Task: Explain a C loop to two audiences: a new student and an experienced programmer. Change the explanation, not the underlying truth.

## Reasoning in more depth
Audience analysis changes which evidence and vocabulary you include. A technician may need a reproducible error and environment; a student may need an immediate workaround; a manager may need impact and options. Keep the underlying facts consistent across versions. A useful message states what happened, who is affected, what is known, what remains uncertain and the next action. Choose the channel by urgency, need for a record and accessibility.

## Independent task | Attempt before reading the answer
Write two versions of a message: a lab login service fails for some students. Address students in one version and the support team in the other. Do not invent a cause.

## Worked answer and marking points
Student version: Some students cannot sign in to the lab service. Save your work locally and report the time and displayed error to support. A cause has not yet been confirmed. Support version: Please investigate intermittent lab sign-in failures. Collect timestamps, affected accounts through an approved private channel, client details and exact errors. Distinguish observed failure from an untested explanation.
